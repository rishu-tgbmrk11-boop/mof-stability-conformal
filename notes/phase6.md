**Which, if any, explains the gap.**
- Not seed variation (section 1b) and not the saved checkpoints (section 1a).
- No single change to the **released** recipe reaches 0.41: target scaling 0.330, quantile features 0.383 (best single run 0.404), feature clipping 0.271; dropping extreme-feature training rows makes it worse. - **The SI's own Table S5 architecture (ReLU, dropout 0.124, hidden 128-512-2048-256-1024, lr 0.002, 100 epochs) trained on standardised temperatures reproduces the SI's thermal numbers**: R2 0.390 (0.024), RMSE 68.4, MAE 48.3 against the SI's 0.41 / 67 / 47, and **13 of 50 runs (26%) have R2 >= 0.41** (best 0.434). Neither ingredient alone does it: the S5 network on raw temperatures gives 0.277, the released network on standardised temperatures 0.330.
- **How far to trust this.** It is a post hoc diagnostic on the SI's own test set, found by trying ten variants; I report all of them and adopt none. What supports it beyond the R2 match is that three SI metrics (R2, RMSE, MAE) are matched at once and that the variation across seeds (sd 0.024) makes 0.41 an ordinary draw. What it cannot show: the SI never mentions target standardisation, the released training script trains on raw temperatures, and other unknown differences could produce the same numbers. The reading that fits all evidence is that the SI's thermal model is the Table S5 network trained on standardised temperatures, **not** the checkpoint or the training script in the release (which give R2 0.14-0.28).

## 2. Joint cascade guarantee (jackknife+ style cross-conformal on the all-three-stable MOFs)

**Joint score.** For a MOF x with stage scores s_1 (thermal: predicted temperature), s_2 (solvent: P(stable)), s_3 (water: P(class 2) + P(class 3)), each stage score is rank-normalised on that stage's calibration data (mid-rank ECDF of the out-of-fold scores of all stage-labelled MOFs in the calibration pool) and the **joint score is the minimum** of the three rank-normalised scores, c(x) = min_k u_k(s_k(x)). A MOF is kept when c(x) is not too low relative to the all-stable MOFs.

**Calibration (CV+ / jackknife+ style).** The calibration positives are the MOFs that are stable at all three stages (17 of the 92 labelled in all three tables, in 14 distinct groups). Each calibration positive i has an out-of-fold joint score c_i from the models trained without its group; for a new MOF x every such fold-model j gives a score c^(j)(x). With n calibration positives, p(x) = (1 + #{i : c_i <= c^(j(i))(x)}) / (n + 1) and **x is kept iff p(x) > alpha** (ties count towards keeping). `src/conformal/joint.py`, tests `tests/test_joint.py`.

**Exact guarantee (what is and is not claimed).**
- Theorem being borrowed: Barber, Candes, Ramdas, Tibshirani (2021), jackknife+ / CV+. For exchangeable calibration and test points and a symmetric fitting procedure, a test point is excluded from the prediction set with probability at most 2 alpha, i.e. **recall >= 1 - 2 alpha** for the population the calibration positives are drawn from (here: MOFs stable at all three stages); K-fold CV+ with equal folds has an extra slack min{2(1-1/K)/(n/K+1), (1-K/n)/(K+1)} (zero for leave-one-out). I adapted the one-sided 'positive-like' form myself and did not re-prove it.
- Conditions that are assumptions here, not facts: (i) the all-stable MOFs are exchangeable (they come from 14 groups with within-group dependence, and are the MOFs that happen to carry all three labels); (ii) the fold models are fit by a symmetric procedure; here each fold leaves out one calibration group (some folds contain two positives) and a random share of the remaining groups, so it is group-level leave-out, not exact leave-one-out; (iii) the test MOFs are exchangeable with the calibration positives (not tested under shift: `notes/phase5.md` section 3).
- Compared with the union bound, the guarantee here is **for the population of interest itself** (the all-stable MOFs), so it does not need the extra assumption that per-stage coverage carries over to that sub-population (`notes/review.md` H3). The cost is that n is only 17 (14 independent groups).

**Largest recall target 17 positives can support.** The rule is non-trivial only if alpha >= 1/(n + 1); below that every MOF is kept.

| calibration units | n | smallest non-trivial alpha | largest guaranteed recall 1 - 2 alpha | largest marginal split-conformal level 1 - alpha |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| all-stable MOFs (positives) | 17 | 0.0556 | 0.889 | 0.944 |
| their groups (conservative) | 14 | 0.0667 | 0.867 | 0.933 |

So with 17 positives the largest recall the jackknife+ guarantee can certify with a non-trivial filter is **0.889** (alpha = 1/18); counted by groups (14) it is 0.867. In the nested evaluation below the calibration pool for each test fold has n = 12-17 positives (median 15), so its smallest non-trivial alpha is higher (about 0.062); the first row of the table is accordingly almost trivial.

**Evaluation (nested, nothing tuned on the test MOFs).** Stage models: random forests (100 trees, fixed in advance; not the Phase 2/3 models, so numbers are not comparable to Fig. 4). Outer: 10 random group folds of all 4,963 MOFs, test = the labelled-in-all-three MOFs of the fold (excluded from every model and from calibration); inner: one fold per calibration positive's group. 3 repetitions (outer seeds 0-2). Each repetition scores each of the 92 labelled MOFs (17 positives, 75 others) once; the three repetitions re-use the same MOFs, so pooled counts (51 positive evaluations) are **not independent**, and Clopper-Pearson intervals below are optimistic.

The union-bound version here is the same cross-conformal rule applied to each stage's own stable MOFs with alpha / 3 per stage (so its stage-wise guarantee, if each stage's coverage carried over to the all-stable MOFs, would also be 1 - 2 alpha, ignoring the K-fold slack and the union over stages' own CV+ error).

**Reading.** (i) Realised recall is above the guaranteed 1 - 2 alpha at every alpha, as expected for a bound that is loose by construction (the 2 alpha factor); at alpha = 0.10 the joint rule has recall 0.961 against a guarantee of 0.80. (ii) At the same alpha the joint score keeps clearly fewer MOFs than the union bound (alpha = 0.10: 0.804 vs 0.891 of the 92) at a somewhat lower, still above-guarantee, recall (0.961 vs 1.000); the union bound spends recall it is not asked for. (iii) Neither rule filters much at a high target: the smallest-alpha rows keep 93-95% of the 92 MOFs, as expected with so few positives. (iv) The kept fraction among MOFs that are not stable at all three stages ('false pass') is high for both rules (0.78 vs 0.88 at alpha = 0.10): with this much calibration noise and weak stage models (group-aware AUC 0.6-0.8) the cascade removes only a modest share of the unstable MOFs. (v) These are 17 MOFs; the data are consistent with the guarantee, but with 17 distinct MOFs (14 groups) and three repetitions of the same MOFs they have little power to contradict it.

### 2b. Calibrating at the group level (the 14 groups as units, not the 17 MOFs)

The 17 all-stable MOFs lie in 14 groups, so 17 overstates the number of independent calibration units. Here each group of all-stable MOFs is **one calibration unit**: its calibration score is the **minimum** of the joint scores of its positives (primary: the lowest score can only keep more MOFs, so this is the conservative choice for recall) or their **mean** (sensitivity); each group is its own fold, so n = number of groups (14 for all 17 positives; 10-14 in the nested folds), and the rule is p(x) = (1 + #{groups g : c_g <= c^(g)(x)}) / (n + 1), keep iff p > alpha. Same nested evaluation as section 2 (the run was repeated to store the group-level p-values; its MOF-level p-values reproduce the first run exactly: True). Both variants were fixed before looking at results.

**Supportable targets.** With 14 units the smallest non-trivial alpha is 1/15 = 0.0667, so the jackknife+ guarantee 1 - 2 alpha can certify at most **0.867** (the MOF-level count of 17 gives 0.889). In the nested folds the pool has as few as 10 groups (smallest non-trivial alpha 0.067 to 0.091), so the first rows are partly trivial.

**Reading.** (i) Counting groups instead of MOFs lowers the largest certifiable target from 0.889 to 0.867. The group-level (minimum) rule keeps at least as many of the 92 MOFs as the MOF-level rule at the listed alphas (more at 6 of 7, equal at 1, fewer at 0; e.g. 0.953 vs 0.819 at alpha = 1/15 and 0.721 vs 0.707 at alpha = 0.15, identical at alpha = 0.10), and its recall is equal or higher, so it is the more conservative rule where the two differ; the differences are up to 12 of the 92 MOFs (at the smallest alpha) and 5 or fewer at the others (the evaluation has 92 MOFs and 51 positive evaluations). (ii) The group-level guarantee is the more defensible statement, because the positives within a group are not independent; the price is a coarser threshold (14 order statistics instead of 17) and a lower ceiling on the certifiable recall. (iii) The mean-of-group variant keeps fewer MOFs than the minimum variant (e.g. 0.772 vs 0.804 at alpha = 0.10), with equal or lower recall, and is not covered by the same conservativeness argument. (iv) As in section 2, recall is above 1 - 2 alpha at every alpha, 17 MOFs / 14 groups give little power to contradict the guarantee, and the repetitions share the same MOFs.

## 3. MOFid groups (and the fingerprint fallback)

### 3.0 Premise check, approvals, what was installed

**Reading.** (i) Adding MOFid to the proxy ('MOFid (full) + proxy') lowers the group-split AUC only slightly, by 0.010 (thermal), 0.032 (solvent), 0.006 (water) in the median, well inside the split-to-split ranges: the proxy already removes most of the leakage that a split by group can remove, and the MOFid duplicates it missed matter little for the model-level numbers. (ii) MOFid **alone** is a much weaker split than the proxy (gap to the random split thermal +0.041, solvent +0.068, water +0.011): it groups exact duplicates but not MOFs of the same family or paper with different building blocks, and leaves the uncovered water MOFs ungrouped. The two are complementary, and the strictest split is their union. (iii) The strictest feasible grouping, fingerprint complete-link 0.25 + proxy, lowers the median AUC further (thermal 0.746, solvent 0.539, water 0.627 against 0.770 / 0.616 / 0.698 for the proxy); its ranges overlap the proxy's, so the direction (every extra link lowers the group-aware score) is clearer than the size. (iv) **The group-aware numbers of Phases 2-5 are therefore somewhat optimistic**: by 0.006-0.032 AUC when MOFid is added to the proxy, and by up to 0.077 with the strictest fingerprint grouping (medians; the split-to-split ranges overlap). The conclusion that group-aware performance is far below random-split performance is unchanged: the paired gap stays positive in at least 28 of 30 splits for every grouping that keeps the proxy links; with MOFid alone the water gap is close to zero (16-18 of 30), consistent with MOFid-only being too weak a split.

## 4. Limitations

- Section 1c: diagnostic only; ten variants were tried on one test set, so a match is evidence of a mechanism, not proof of what the authors did. The SI's missing details (batch size, patience, target handling, the 190 / 179 feature lists) are exactly where a different pipeline could hide.
- Section 2: random-forest stage models (not the Phase 2/3 networks or trees); the 17 positives are 14 groups and are the MOFs that happen to carry all three labels; three repetitions share the same MOFs; guarantee is borrowed from the jackknife+/CV+ theorem with group-level leave-out and is an assumption under exchangeability, not tested under shift; no result on the unlabelled MOFs of the screening population.
- Section 3: MOFid-v1 (2019 code, topology unknown for about half of the MOFs, where only building blocks identify the MOF); coverage 86.6% overall but only 39% for water; the CoRE 2019 CIFs are ASR (solvent-removed) structures that may differ from the structures behind the labels; MOFid duplicates are exact-chemistry duplicates, not near-duplicates (same linker, different metal). '-only' groupings leave all uncovered MOFs as singletons, which understates leakage for water; '-proxy' groupings keep the proxy's links.
- Group metrics in 3.3 use random forests (not the released networks) and 30 splits per grouping.
