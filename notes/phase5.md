## 3. Family-shift validation (leave-clusters-out)

**Design (`p5_03`).** Per stage, MOFs are embedded without labels (z-score, PCA 20) and clustered with k-means (K = 10); whole clusters go to **test** (about 20% of MOFs), other whole clusters to **calibration** (about 20%), the rest to **training**; a random forest (300 trees, fixed in advance; thermal = regressor on temperature, solvent/water = classifier, score = P(stable)) is trained on the training clusters, the per-stage split-conformal recall rule is calibrated on the calibration clusters, and recall is measured on the test clusters. Repeated 20 times (new k-means seed and cluster order). **Control:** the same models with calibration and test both drawn (random halves) from the test clusters, which are exchangeable inside the cluster. The 20 repetitions share MOFs, so they are not independent; 'share of reps >= target' is descriptive.

## 4. The cascade claim and the sensitivity of the 17

**Wording now used.** Each stage's conformal guarantee is for that stage's own stable MOFs. It does **not** imply the same miss probability for the MOFs that are stable at all three stages, so 'recall >= 1 - alpha for MOFs stable at all three stages' holds under an **assumption**, not as a proved property. The 17 all-stable MOFs cannot test the assumption, and their out-of-fold scores are lower than those of the other stage-stable MOFs for thermal and water (`notes/review.md` H3). The observed cascade recall (0.94-0.98 on the 17) is consistent with the target but does not confirm it. Simulation (review S7c): if the all-stable MOFs are 0.5 sd 'harder' in every stage, the union-bound cascade's recall is 0.78 at target 0.90.

## 5. Group-vs-random gaps as ranges over many splits

Split seed s gives one random **group** split (`random_group_assign`: all groups shuffled, cut at 60/20/20 by MOF count) and one MOF-level random split (`random_assign`), both over the union of the three tables; the same model family is trained on each (60%) and scored on the test part (about 20%). **Gap = random - group, paired by split seed** (for MAE: group - random). Cells: mean [2.5%, 97.5% percentile over splits] (min-max). The earlier single-split gaps (`phase2.md` section 2) are single draws from these distributions.

## 6. What changed in the conclusions

- The SI's solvent and water numbers are reproduced by retraining on the SI's own split (solvent only with the as-coded early-stopping behaviour); thermal R2 0.41 is not. For solvent and water the earlier 'paper vs release' discrepancy is explained by which split was used; for thermal it is not (R2 0.41 remains unreproduced, cause unknown).
- Those SI numbers come from a test set where about half of the MOFs have a same-group MOF in training, and the group-clean half scores much lower (section 1d).
- The plain conformal recall rule is valid for exchangeable calibration/test MOFs but falls short under cluster shift; the one distance-aware variant tried does not fix this. The stacked-filter recall guarantee holds only under assumptions that were not met in the shift experiment (section 3) and are untestable for the all-stable population (section 4).
- The group-vs-random gap is robustly positive but its size is a distribution, not a number (section 5).

## 7. Limitations of this phase

- Section 1 uses the released feature columns (170 / 170 / 159), not the SI's 190 / 190 / 179, and the SI Table S5 variants use released batch size and patience; the thermal gap to the SI could come from these unknowns, from different data versions, or from something not documented. I did not investigate further.
- Section 1d compares a leaky and a clean half of one test set; the halves differ in composition. The group definition is the refcode-family + DOI proxy.
- Section 3 uses random forests (not the released networks) and one clustering recipe; the shortfall may differ for other models, cluster counts or embeddings, and a real deployment shift (new data source, later years) may be larger or smaller than k-means cluster shift.
- Section 5's NN ranges rest on 10 splits; the RF ranges on 100, with a different model class from the released networks.
