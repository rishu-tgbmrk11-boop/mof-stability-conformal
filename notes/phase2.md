# Phase 2: group-aware splits, honest retraining, and conformal recall control

Everything below is produced by `scripts/p2_01..p2_05` (outputs in `data/processed/phase2/`); this file is generated by `scripts/p2_05_write_notes.py`, so every measured number is read from a saved result. Reusable code: `src/features/groups.py`, `src/features/stability_data.py`, `src/models/stages.py`, `src/eval/metrics.py`, `src/conformal/recall.py`, with tests in `tests/`.

## 0. Design choices and caveats (read first)

- **Groups are proxies.** The released data has no MOFid (see `notes/stability_inventory.md`), so groups are connected components over *4-letter CSD refcode family* and *DOI* (DOIs exist only for the 2,179 solvent-table MOFs). MOFs that are the same material under different refcodes and without a shared DOI can still end up in different groups, so some leakage may remain.
- **One split for all three tasks.** Groups are built on the union of the three tables (4,963 MOFs) and each group is assigned once to train / calibration / test (60/20/20 by MOF count, seed 0), so a MOF has the same role in every task.
- **Smaller training sets than the paper.** 60% of MOFs train each model (the released scripts use 80%); 20% is held out for calibration and 20% for test. The 'random' comparison uses the same 60/20/20 sizes and the same code, with MOF-level random assignment, so the two columns differ only in how MOFs are assigned.
- **Features come from each task's own CSV** (not the merged `all_in_one` table whose substitutions the audit found), using the released feature columns (170 / 170 / 159).
- **Honest training:** the early-stopping restore is fixed (`copy.deepcopy` of the best state; unit-tested to return exactly the best-epoch weights). The 10% validation set used for early stopping is also held out by group. Released architectures and hyperparameters are used unchanged (thermal and solvent networks, 5 seeds); the water forest uses the released best parameters (200 trees, no depth limit, bootstrap off) and was **not re-tuned** with group-aware cross-validation.
- **'Stable' is defined as in the paper's screening (SI Section 4):** thermal decomposition temperature >= 350 degC; solvent label +1; water class 2 or 3 (0-based). Stage scores for conformal control: thermal = predicted temperature, solvent = predicted probability, water = P(class 2) + P(class 3).

## 3. Split conformal recall control per stage

**Method** (`src/conformal/recall.py`). For a stage with score s (higher = more stable), keep a MOF if s >= t. With the n calibration MOFs that are *truly stable* (labels known), take t = the k-th smallest of their scores, k = floor(alpha (n + 1)). If a new stable MOF's score is exchangeable with those calibration scores, the chance that it falls below t is k/(n + 1) <= alpha, so **expected recall >= 1 - alpha** (expectation over the calibration draw; ties only make it conservative; if alpha (n + 1) < 1 nothing can be filtered). The calibration MOFs are whole groups held out from training; exchangeability is therefore at the group level, and the guarantee is marginal over random assignments of groups to calibration/test, not a statement about one particular partition.

- The PAC variant raises that to 71.0%-84.8% (group-aware) at the cost of keeping a few percent more MOFs. **It does not reach the nominal 90% in every cell**; this check measures recall on a finite test set (139-313 stable MOFs per test set, which adds sampling noise) and, in the group-aware case, within-group dependence also violates the i.i.d. assumption behind the Beta-distribution argument, so I do not claim the PAC property is empirically verified.

The paper's fixed solvent rule (p > 0.9) keeps only 12.0% of the test MOFs and recovers only 14.6% of the truly stable ones.

## 4. Cascade with a union bound

**Rule.** A MOF passes the cascade if it passes all three stages, each calibrated at level alpha_k = alpha / 3. If each stage misses a truly stable MOF with probability at most alpha_k, the union bound gives P(rejected somewhere) <= alpha_1 + alpha_2 + alpha_3 = alpha, i.e. recall >= 1 - alpha for MOFs that are stable at all three stages. **Assumption to flag:** each stage's guarantee is for the stage's own stable MOFs (the calibration positives), not for the sub-population that is stable at all three stages, so applying the bound to that sub-population assumes that coverage carries over to it; with 17 such MOFs this cannot be tested statistically.

**Evaluation design.** Only 92 MOFs carry all three labels and just 17 are stable at all three stages. To score them out of sample, the groups are split into K = 5 folds; for each fold the three stages are trained (75% of the other groups) and calibrated (25%) on the other folds' groups and predict the held-out fold, so every MOF has out-of-fold scores and thresholds (this is repeated for 5 different fold assignments, `p2_04_cascade.py`). Models use training seed 0.

**Honest reading.**
- With the union bound the observed recall on the 17 all-stable MOFs is at or above the target at every level (mean 0.953, 0.941, 0.929, 0.847 for alpha = 0.05, 0.1, 0.2, 0.3), but with only 17 positives the 95% interval is wide (for example 16 of 17 passing gives [0.71, 1.00]): **the data are consistent with the guarantee, and cannot confirm it.**
- Without the union bound the cascade falls below target as alpha grows (mean recall 0.941, 0.847, 0.729, 0.435 against targets 0.95, 0.90, 0.80, 0.70), which is the compounding of stage misses that the union bound is there to control. The union bound is conservative: it keeps more MOFs than needed.
- The cost is large: at a target recall of 0.95 the cascade keeps 92.2% of the labelled MOFs and lets through 91.5% of those that are not stable at all three stages; even at a target of 0.70 it keeps 69.8%.
- The five repetitions re-use the same 92 MOFs (same 17 positives), so their spread reflects model/calibration randomness only, not new data; they are not five independent tests.
- The paper's fixed rules pass almost none of the all-stable MOFs when the models are trained on unseen groups, consistent with the solvent rule (p > 0.9) alone recovering only 14.6% of stable MOFs in section 3; I did not run a per-stage attribution here.

## 5. Limitations and open questions

- **Proxy groups, not MOFid groups.** Remaining same-material leakage across groups would make every group-aware number optimistic. The next step for a stricter test is MOFid (needs CIFs; large download, ask first).
- **Recall is measured against the released labels**, which may contain noise; it is not a statement about ground truth.
- **The cascade evaluation rests on 17 positives** from the 92 MOFs that happen to carry all three labels; they may not represent the screening population.
- **The water forest was not re-tuned** under group-aware CV; the neural networks use the released hyperparameters, which were tuned (if at all) on a random split.
- **Training fraction 60%** instead of 80% lowers all models somewhat relative to the paper's protocol; the random-split column shows that effect separately from the group effect.
- **No external test set.** All numbers come from the released tables.
