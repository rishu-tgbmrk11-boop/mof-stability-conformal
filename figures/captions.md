## Figure 1 (`fig1_group_vs_random`)
**Group-aware splitting lowers the measured performance of the stability models.** Test performance of the released model architectures retrained on a random (MOF-level, grey squares) or a group-aware (orange circles) 60/20/20 split with identical sizes and code (Phase 2 models, training seed 0). (a) AUC for the 'truly stable' call; (b) accuracy of the stable / not-stable call; (c) thermal mean absolute error (°C). Test sets: group-aware thermal 589 MOFs in 385 groups, solvent 425 MOFs in 225 groups, water 232 MOFs in 125 groups; random-split test MOFs: thermal 625, solvent 421, water 226. Error bars are 95% percentile bootstrap intervals (1000 resamples) over test groups for the group-aware split and over individual MOFs for the random split (the two kinds of interval are not directly comparable). The group-aware test sets differ from the random ones, so differences are indicative only; one split, one seed.

## Figure 2 (`fig2_recall_vs_target`)
**Realised recall of the per-stage conformal threshold.** Realised recall on the group-aware test set against the target recall 1 − α, for the thermal (a), solvent (b) and water (c) stages; grey squares: Phase 2 models (released hyperparameters), orange circles: Phase 3 models (tree-model candidates chosen by group cross-validation on the training groups only); dashed line: target. Thresholds are the k-th smallest calibration-positive score, k = floor(α (n+1)), from the group-aware calibration set (thermal 648 MOFs in 414 groups, solvent 443 MOFs in 238 groups, water 215 MOFs in 117 groups) and applied to the test sets (thermal 589 MOFs in 385 groups, solvent 425 MOFs in 225 groups, water 232 MOFs in 125 groups). Shaded bands are 95% percentile bootstrap intervals (1000 resamples) over test groups with the threshold held fixed; they show test-set sampling noise only, not the variability of the calibration draw. Each point is one calibration draw (split seed 0), so single points can lie below the diagonal even though the guarantee is for the average over draws.

## Figure 3 (`fig3_kept_vs_recall`)
**Fraction of MOFs kept at each target recall.** Share of all group-aware test MOFs that pass the conformal threshold of each stage (thermal a, solvent b, water c) against the target recall; same models, thresholds and test sets as Fig. 2 (test sets: thermal 589 MOFs in 385 groups, solvent 425 MOFs in 225 groups, water 232 MOFs in 125 groups). Dotted line: a filter without information (kept = recall). Shaded bands: 95% percentile bootstrap intervals (1000 resamples) over test groups, threshold fixed. Lower is better at equal realised recall, but the two model sets have different realised recalls at the same target, so compare them at equal realised recall (see Fig. 2).

## Figure 4 (`fig4_cascade`)
**Stacked thermal, solvent and water filters (cascade) on the MOFs with all three labels.** (a) Recall on the 17 MOFs that are stable at all three stages, against the target recall 1 − α. (b) Fraction of the 92 MOFs carrying all three labels (66 proxy groups) that pass all three filters. Orange circles: Phase 3 models with the union bound (each stage calibrated at α/3); grey squares: Phase 2 models with the union bound; green triangles: Phase 3 models without the union bound (each stage at α). Scores and thresholds are out-of-fold: groups are split into K = 5 folds, three stages are trained and calibrated on the other folds' groups, and the procedure is repeated for 5 fold assignments (small dots in (a)); markers are means over the repetitions. Error bars are 95% Clopper-Pearson intervals for the rounded mean count of passes out of 17 and do **not** account for the repetitions, which re-use the same MOFs and are not independent. With only 17 positives the intervals are wide: the data are consistent with the guarantee but cannot confirm it. Phase 3 configurations were chosen on the training groups of the single split, which gives a small selection leak into these folds. The 17 all-stable MOFs are those with thermal >= 350 °C, solvent label +1 and water class 2 or 3 (a different cut-off gives 3-49). The union bound is **not proved** for this population: each stage's guarantee covers that stage's own stable MOFs, and the all-stable MOFs score lower than the other stage-stable MOFs for thermal and water.

## Figure 5 (`fig5_price_of_recall`)
**The price of recall: fraction of MOFs kept against discriminative power (AUC).** For each stage (thermal a, solvent b, water c) and target recall 0.80 / 0.90 / 0.95 (colours), the fraction of all MOFs that must be kept so that this fraction of the truly stable MOFs is kept, with the threshold known exactly (isolating discrimination from calibration noise). Lines: analytic binormal model at the stage's prevalence. Small squares: **simulated** scores (negatives N(0,1), positives N(d,1)) on each stage's real labels and group ids, one dataset per AUC value, plotted at the sample AUC; they are not measured. Open squares and filled circles: **real** Phase 2 and Phase 3 stage models. Real and synthetic points use the pooled group-aware calibration and test MOFs (thermal 1237 MOFs in 799 groups, solvent 868 MOFs in 463 groups, water 447 MOFs in 242 groups), seed-0 models. Bars are 95% percentile bootstrap intervals (1000 resamples) over groups (both axes for the real models). Simulated scores are independent of group, which is optimistic, and real score distributions need not be binormal.
